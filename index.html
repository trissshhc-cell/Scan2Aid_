YOUR MEDKIT • 5 ESSENTIALS

First aid, within reach.

A practical guide to using your first-aid kit safely.

Important safety notice

For minor first aid only. This guide is not a substitute for professional medical care. Seek qualified medical attention for deep wounds, burns, allergic reactions or worsening symptoms (such as fever, pus or spreading redness). For severe bleeding, breathing difficulty or facial/throat swelling, get emergency help immediately.

01

Bandage scissors

How to use

  1. Wash your hands. Use clean, blunt-tip bandage scissors.
  2. Cut tape or gauze away from the body when possible.
  3. If cutting a loose bandage or clothing near skin, keep the blunt tip toward the skin and make sure the skin is clear of the blades. Cut slowly.
  4. Clean and dry the scissors after use.

Caution: Never cut skin, stuck dressings or material you cannot safely see.

02

Betadine (povidone-iodine)

How to use

  1. Wash hands and stop bleeding first. Rinse a minor wound with clean running water.
  2. Check the expiry date and use only a skin/wound formulation. Do not use if allergic to povidone-iodine or any ingredient.
  3. Clean the surrounding skin; apply to the minor wound only if your specific product label permits it. Follow its directions and let it dry before covering.
  4. Ask a pharmacist before use in pregnancy, thyroid disease or young children.

Caution: Clean water is usually enough. Avoid eyes, deep wounds and large areas. Stop if irritation occurs.

03

Sterile gauze

How to use

  1. Wash hands and wear gloves if available. Open a sterile gauze packet without touching the wound-contact surface.
  2. Press firmly and continuously on a bleeding minor wound. If blood soaks through, add another pad without removing the first.
  3. Once bleeding stops, rinse the wound, then cover with fresh sterile gauze and secure with tape or a bandage.
  4. Replace the dressing when wet, dirty or loose.

Caution: For heavy or uncontrolled bleeding, get emergency help and maintain pressure. Do not remove embedded objects; press around them.

04

Plaster (adhesive bandage)

How to use

  1. Wash hands, stop any bleeding, rinse the small cut or scrape and dry the surrounding skin.
  2. Choose a plaster large enough to cover the wound or intact blister. Do not burst a blister.
  3. Peel off the backing without touching the pad. Center the pad over the wound and stick the ends to healthy skin.
  4. Change daily or whenever wet, dirty or loose.

Caution: Do not put adhesive directly on the wound or wrap tightly around a finger.

05

Elastic bandage

How to use

  1. Rest the injured area. If there is a wound, cover it with sterile gauze first.
  2. For a minor sprain, start below the injured area and wrap toward the body, overlapping each turn by about half the bandage width.
  3. Keep the wrap snug, not tight. Secure with tape or the supplied fastener; leave fingers or toes visible.
  4. Check circulation immediately and regularly. Loosen or remove if there is numbness, tingling, increased pain, swelling, or pale, blue or cold fingers or toes.

Caution: Seek care if circulation does not return promptly, the limb looks deformed, or you cannot use it normally.
